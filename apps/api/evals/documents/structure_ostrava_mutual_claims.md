# Ostrava Mutual Claims Handling Standard

Ostrava Mutual is a fictional mid-size property and motor insurer, and this document was written as evaluation material for an ontology import test.

Document owner: Head of Claims
Approved by: Claims Committee, September 2026

## 1 Purpose and Scope

This standard sets out how Ostrava Mutual handles claims under its
property and motor policies, from the first contact with the customer
to the closure of the file. Ostrava Mutual operates Claims handling as
a single end-to-end process owned by the Head of Claims, and it
operates Delegated authority as the framework that limits what each
handler may reserve, offer or pay.

This standard applies to every claim notified under a personal or commercial property policy or a private or fleet motor policy issued by Ostrava Mutual, whether notified by the policyholder, by a broker acting under a terms-of-business agreement, by a third party or by an assistance provider, and it applies equally to employees, to contracted loss adjusters and to any outsourced claims administrator acting on our behalf; where a local procedure conflicts with this standard, this standard prevails unless the Claims Committee has approved a written exception, which must be recorded on the exceptions register and reviewed at least once a year.

## 2 Claims Handling Lifecycle

Claims handling at Ostrava Mutual includes the stages below, each described in its own section of this standard
- First notification of loss
- Triage
- Assessment
- Settlement
- Recovery
- Fraud referral, which may be raised at any stage of the claim and suspends settlement until the Special Investigations Unit has concluded its review

## 3 First Notification of Loss

First notification of loss is the first contact in which the customer,
a broker or a third party tells us that an insured event has occurred.
First notification of loss accepts notifications through the notification channels
listed below, and it includes loss registration, which must be completed
within one working day of the first contact.

Where the notification is received more than thirty days after the date of loss, or where the customer cannot provide a reasonable account of the circumstances, the policy number, the location of the loss and the identity of any third party involved, the handler must nevertheless register the claim, must not express any view on liability or coverage during the call, and must record the reasons for the delay in the claim notes so that the triage handler can weigh them when the claim is scored; a claim must never be refused at notification.

### 3.1 Notification Channels

- Telephone notification to the claims line, staffed 24 hours a day for motor claims
- Online claim form
- Broker notification
- Assistance line referral, used when a roadside or home emergency provider has attended the loss

### 3.2 Loss Registration

Loss registration consists of the numbered steps below and is completed by the claims handler who takes the notification
1. Policy verification against the policy administration system, confirming that the policy was in force on the date of loss
2. Coverage check, in which the handler reads the policy wording and schedule and records any exclusion, condition precedent or endorsement that may apply
3. Claim number allocation
4. Acknowledgement letter issued to the policyholder, and to the broker where one is appointed, within two working days of notification

## 4 Triage

Triage allocates every registered claim to one of the handling tracks
below within one working day of registration, using the triage score
produced by the claims system and the judgement of the triage handler.
Triage applies the fraud indicators to every claim before a track is set.

- Fast track, for motor glass, simple theft of contents and property claims below CZK 50,000 where liability is clear
- Standard track
- Complex loss track, for claims above CZK 500,000, bodily injury, subsidence, commercial business interruption or any claim with a coverage dispute

The fast track includes straight-through payment, in which the claims system pays the claim without manual assessment once the coverage check is passed.

On the complex loss track the handling team must, without waiting for the customer to request it, arrange a loss adjuster appointment within forty-eight hours of triage, agree a claims plan with the customer that states the next steps and the expected timetable, set an initial reserve that reflects the most likely outcome rather than the amount claimed, and carry out a reserve review at least every thirty days or sooner whenever new information changes the expected cost by more than twenty per cent; the team leader signs off each reserve review in the claims system.

## 5 Assessment

Assessment establishes whether the loss is covered, what caused it and
what it will cost to put right. Assessment includes motor assessment and
property assessment, and the assessor is chosen according to the triage
track and the size of the loss.

### 5.1 Motor Assessment

Motor assessment consists of the procedure below, carried out by an approved engineer
1. Vehicle inspection at the repairer or through a video assessment
2. Total loss evaluation, where the repair cost exceeds sixty per cent of the pre-accident value
3. Repair authorisation issued to the approved repairer

The vehicle inspection produces a repair estimate and photo evidence of all damage, and both are attached to the claim file before any repair authorisation is issued.

### 5.2 Property Assessment

Property assessment consists of the procedure below, carried out by a claims surveyor or a loss adjuster
1. Site visit
2. Emergency repairs to make the property safe, watertight and secure
3. Scope of works agreed with the customer and the contractor

The site visit includes a moisture survey for every escape-of-water and flood claim.

## 6 Settlement

Settlement follows the steps in the table below. No step may be skipped,
and the handler records the date each step is completed so that the
cycle time can be reported to the Claims Committee each month.

| Step | Activity | Owner | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Liability decision | Claims handler | Decision letter |
| 2 | Quantum agreement | Claims handler or loss adjuster | Agreed loss figure |
| 3 | Settlement offer | Handler within their authority | Offer letter |
| 4 | Payment release | Claims payments team | Payment confirmation |
| 5 | Claim closure | Claims handler | Closed file |

A settlement offer must never exceed the delegated authority of the person who makes it.

### 6.1 Settlement Methods

Settlement offers one of the settlement methods below, and the handler prefers repair in kind wherever a suitable repairer or contractor is available
- Cash settlement
- Repair in kind through an approved repairer or contractor
- Replacement in kind through a panel supplier

A total loss payment is a kind of cash settlement, calculated on the pre-accident value of the vehicle less the policy excess.

Payment is made only to the policyholder, to a loss payee noted on the schedule, or to a repairer, contractor or supplier appointed through the settlement methods above, and the payments team must not release any payment where the payee's bank details were changed within the previous fourteen days, where the payee differs from the name on the policy without a signed mandate, or where an open fraud referral exists on the claim, until the team leader has verified the change by a call-back to a number already held on file; every such verification is recorded.

## 7 Recovery

Recovery returns to Ostrava Mutual the money it has paid where another
party is responsible for the loss or where the damaged property keeps a
value. Recovery includes subrogation, salvage and excess recovery, and
the recovery team reports the amounts recovered to finance each month.

### 7.1 Subrogation

Subrogation relies on the liability decision to identify the responsible party, and consists of the following
1. Third-party identification
2. Letter of claim to the third party or their insurer
3. Recovery proceedings where the letter of claim is not answered within ninety days

### 7.2 Salvage

Salvage covers every vehicle declared a total loss and every item of property replaced in kind, and the handler must not agree to leave salvage with the customer unless the salvage value is deducted from the settlement; salvage is disposed of through the salvage auction run by our contracted salvage agent, and the total loss evaluation triggers the salvage auction automatically once the customer has accepted the total loss payment and signed the transfer of ownership, with the proceeds credited to the claim within thirty days.

### 7.3 Excess Recovery

Excess recovery seeks the policyholder's uninsured excess from the responsible party alongside our own recovery.

## 8 Fraud Referral

Fraud referral is the route by which any employee, adjuster or
partner raises a concern that a claim may be dishonest. A referral
never delays the customer being told what happens next, and it never
leads to a claim being declined without evidence.
Fraud referral includes the fraud indicators and the SIU investigation.

### 8.1 Fraud Indicators

The fraud indicators below must lead to a referral when two or more are present on the same claim, whatever the claim value, and the triage handler records which indicators were considered
- Late notification
- Inconsistent account of the circumstances between the customer, witnesses and the physical evidence
- Recent policy inception, meaning a loss within sixty days of the policy start or of a cover increase
- Prior claims history

### 8.2 SIU Investigation

The SIU investigation consists of the steps below and is carried out by the Special Investigations Unit, which reports to the Head of Claims and not to the handling team
1. Desktop review of the claim file, open-source records and industry databases
2. Recorded interview with the customer, held with consent and with a copy of the recording offered
3. Field investigation

The SIU investigation informs the liability decision, and only the Claims Manager may decline a claim on the grounds of fraud.

## 9 Delegated Authority

Delegated authority sets the highest amount that each role may reserve,
offer or pay on a single claim. Authority is personal, is granted in
writing by the Head of Claims and lapses when the holder changes role.
Delegated authority includes the five tiers in the table below.

| Tier | Authority | Holder | Maximum settlement in CZK | Also may |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Desk authority | Claims handler | 150,000 | Approve fast track payments |
| 2 | Senior handler authority | Senior claims handler | 750,000 | Appoint loss adjusters |
| 3 | Team leader authority | Claims team leader | 2,500,000 | Sign off reserve reviews |
| 4 | Claims manager authority | Claims manager | 10,000,000 | Decline claims for fraud |
| 5 | Claims committee authority | Claims Committee | Unlimited | Approve exceptions to this standard |

Where a claim exceeds the authority of the handler who holds it, the handler must refer it upwards to the next tier before making any offer, reserve change or payment commitment, and no person may split a claim into several payments, settle related claims separately or agree an interim payment for the purpose of staying within their own authority; any breach of delegated authority is reported to the Claims Committee and may lead to the authority being withdrawn.
