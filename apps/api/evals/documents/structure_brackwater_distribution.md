# Order-to-cash and warehouse procedures

Wiki export | Space: Operations | Page owner: Head of Operations | Last edited 14 August 2026 | Status: Approved

Brackwater Distribution is a fictional organisation and this document was written as evaluation material for a document import test.

## About this page

Brackwater Distribution is a wholesale distributor of electrical supplies (cable, switchgear, lighting,
containment and tools) serving contractors, installers and facilities teams from five depots. This page is
the working copy of how we take an order, check that the customer can pay, pick and send the goods, handle
anything that comes back and bill for it. Where this page and a local depot note disagree, this page wins
until the process owner says otherwise.

## Depot network

The depot network is run as one stock pool with local counters, so an order taken in one depot can be picked in another.

- Brinmoor depot (head office and national distribution centre)
- Saltcote depot
- Hallowford depot
- Kestle depot
- Tidewell depot (cable cutting centre)

## Systems

- Customer master: one record per account, holding credit limit, payment terms, delivery addresses and price group
- Warehouse management system (WMS): bins, stock levels, pick lists, replenishment tasks and cycle counts
- Finance ledger: invoices, credit notes, cash receipts and the aged debt report

Only the credit controller and the finance clerk may change credit fields in the customer master.

## Order intake

Order intake is owned by the sales administrator at each depot. Orders reach us through four order channels:

- Trade counter
- Web shop
- EDI orders from the larger contractors, received every fifteen minutes
- Telesales

Every order, whatever the channel, goes through order validation before it is released to the warehouse, and no one may skip it for a known customer, a rush job or a counter sale that looks routine; validation is where we catch the wrong price group, the closed account and the item we cannot supply, and it is far cheaper to catch those at the desk than to reverse a picked, dispatched and invoiced order at month end, which happened more than once last year and cost the depots a great deal of goodwill.

### Order validation

1. Customer lookup against the customer master, confirming the account is open and the delivery address is on file
2. Price check against the customer's price group and any live project price
3. Stock availability check across all five depots, reserving stock in the depot nearest the delivery address

A stock availability check that cannot cover the full quantity creates a backorder for the balance.

## Credit control

Credit control protects the business from bad debt without stopping good customers from trading. The
credit controller owns the policy and the credit desk runs the daily checks. Credit control includes the
credit check on every order, the credit hold when an account is over its limit or overdue, and the
credit limit review, done for each account every twelve months or after two late payments.

### Credit check

The credit check reads the customer master for the credit limit, payment terms and account status.

| Step | Activity | Owner | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Account status check | Credit desk | Stop flag or clear |
| 2 | Exposure calculation | Credit desk | Exposure report |
| 3 | Credit decision | Credit desk | Release or hold |

### Credit hold

An order that fails the credit check goes on credit hold and stays in the held-orders queue, where it cannot be picked, cannot join a wave and cannot be invoiced, until the credit controller or, for accounts with a limit under five thousand pounds, a named member of the credit desk approves a hold release, which must record the reason, the person who authorised it and any payment promise taken from the customer, because the aged debt review at month end reads these notes line by line.

## Picking and dispatch

- Wave planning
- Picking
- Packing
- Dispatch

Picking and dispatch is owned by the warehouse supervisor at each depot.

### Wave planning

Waves are planned by the warehouse supervisor at 07:00, 11:00 and 14:00, grouped by delivery route. A wave closes thirty minutes before its van departs.

### Picking

Pick lists are generated by the warehouse management system when a wave is released. Zone picking is a type of picking used at Brinmoor, where each picker stays in one aisle zone and totes travel on the conveyor. Batch picking is a type of picking used at the smaller depots, where one picker collects several orders in a single pass.

### Packing

1. Packing check against the pick list, line by line, with any short pick reported to the supervisor
2. Labelling with the route code and drop number
3. Loading in reverse drop order, heaviest items on the floor of the van

### Dispatch

Dispatch prints a delivery note for every drop and the driver captures proof of delivery on the
handheld at the customer's site, with a photograph where nobody is there to sign. Proof of delivery
triggers the invoice run for that order, and nothing is invoiced without it.

## Returns

- Return authorisation
- Returns inspection
- Credit note

Returns are accepted only with a return authorisation raised by the depot sales administrator, and the return authorisation issues an RMA number that must be written on the outside of every carton, because goods that arrive without an RMA number are refused at the goods-in door, which frustrates some customers but protects stock accuracy, and the only exception, a contractor handing back a faulty isolator at the trade counter, is logged by the counter staff on the same day.

### Returns inspection

Returned goods are inspected within two working days and given one disposition:

| Disposition | When | Stock effect |
|---|---|---|
| Restock | Unused, sealed, current range | Back to pickable stock |
| Scrap | Damaged or used | Written off |
| Return to supplier | Faulty within supplier warranty | Held in quarantine bay |

Accepted returns raise a credit note, and the credit note reverses the original sales invoice in full or in part.

## Invoicing

- Invoice run
- Dunning
- Cash allocation

### Invoice run

1. Invoice generation for every order with proof of delivery, producing one sales invoice per delivery
2. Invoice dispatch by email, or by post for the accounts flagged in the customer master

The invoice run posts every invoice to the finance ledger at 18:00 each working day.

### Dunning

Dunning starts seven days after the due date with a first reminder letter, followed by a second reminder letter at twenty-one days and a telephone call from the credit desk at thirty days, and when dunning reaches forty-five days it triggers a credit hold on the account, which the credit controller may lift only once the overdue balance is paid or a payment plan is signed, while the dunning history stays on the customer record for the next review.

### Cash allocation

Cash allocation matches each receipt to open invoices on the day it lands in the bank. The finance
clerk allocates by remittance advice first and by oldest invoice second, and parks anything that
cannot be matched in the suspense account for no more than five working days.

## Warehouse operations

- Goods receipt
- Replenishment
- Cycle counting

### Goods receipt

1. Receipt check against the purchase order, counting cartons and checking seals
2. Putaway to the bin suggested by the WMS within four hours of receipt

### Replenishment

Replenishment tasks are raised by the warehouse management system when a pick face falls below its minimum.

### Cycle counting

Cycle counting replaces the annual stocktake at all five depots. Counts are blind, so the counter
never sees the expected quantity, and the warehouse supervisor signs off each count sheet before
the adjustment is posted.

Every bin is counted at least four times a year, high-value lines such as switchgear, armoured cable and consumer units are counted monthly, and any variance over two hundred pounds or five percent of the bin value is recounted by a second person before adjustment, investigated with the pick history for the last thirty days and reported in the weekly depot meeting, because a stock error found at the bin is always cheaper than one found by a customer.

## Key measures

- On-time in-full, target 97 percent
- Days sales outstanding, target 42 days
- Credit hold release time, target four working hours
- Stock record accuracy, target 98.5 percent
